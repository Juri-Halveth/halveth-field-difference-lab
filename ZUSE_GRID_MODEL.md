# Zuse grid question — a bounded software experiment

The [interactive research room](zuse.html) visualizes both declared rules from this note in the browser. It links each historical statement to the source used for that statement and presents Juri Janovski's new code as a separate 2026 contribution.

Konrad Zuse's 1967 article ["Rechnender Raum" (reprinted by *Spektrum der Wissenschaft*)](https://www.spektrum.de/pdf/rechnenderraum-pdf/1030307?file=) discusses how a pulse advancing across four neighbours of a Cartesian grid can acquire preferred directions. We use that historical **question** to test whether a local model's observation depends on the chosen representation. The [Konrad Zuse Internet Archive index](https://zuse.zib.de/pdfs) also lists earlier notes on program storage, cellular automata and the computing space; their titles are metadata, not a specification of this implementation.

The [Deutsches Museum's finding aid for Zuse's papers](https://www.deutsches-museum.de/assets/Forschung/Archiv/Download/Findbuecher_PDF/NL_207_Zuse.pdf) records a 25 February 1956 patent application for a machine calculating over grid-arranged field values (patent no. 1122748, granted 25 January 1962). This is specific historical prior work on grid calculations. The finding aid alone does not establish that its hardware, update rule or output is equivalent to the experiment below.

`experiments/zuse-lattice.js` defines two bounded models of our own. One unit begins at `(0,0)`. In the first rule, each occupied cell passes one quarter of its value north, south, east and west. In the comparison rule, each of the eight adjacent cells receives `1/12` and the source cell retains `1/3`. The state is a map from integer coordinates to values. These deterministic distribution rules conserve total value within floating-point tolerance and have no physical units. The comparison rule is our declared control, not a reconstruction of Zuse's hardware.

Run the checks with `npm test`. After four steps, **both** rules have mass `1` and `E[x²] = E[y²] = 2`. Their joint structure differs: the four-neighbour rule has no mass at `(4,4)`, while the comparison rule does. Their mixed fourth moments `E[x²y²]` are respectively `3` and `13/3`. Thus matching the two coordinate variances does not identify the propagation rule. The four-neighbour frontier reaches radius `4` on an axis and `√8` on a diagonal; the comparison rule can also reach `(4,4)`. Same seed, horizon and per-axis second moment do **not** mean identical maximum step distance or equivalent physics. These tests check only the declared software models.

The second rule now supplies a discriminating software comparison. A stronger comparison would bind an observable to a particular measurement process and choose a threshold before seeing the result. Any comparison with measured physics would additionally require an instrument, units, calibration, competing predictions and uncertainty. This code makes no claim about actual spacetime, gravity, digital particles or an exploitable security defect. Zuse's own [1967 article](https://www.spektrum.de/pdf/rechnenderraum-pdf/1030307?file=) says that his investigations at that stage were carried out on paper and called for further computation; our software probe does not retroactively turn that historical proposal into an empirical result.

## Four corners inside the original field

The yellow outline in the first release was a **measurement target** at `(step, step)`, not an entity. The October 31 correction follows Juri's screenshot: four persistent model bodies start at the symmetric corners `(4,4)`, `(-4,4)`, `(-4,-4)`, `(4,-4)` **on the original blue four-neighbour canvas**. The first one keeps the earlier yellow look. Four paired counter-roles (`COUNTER/NE`, `COUNTER/NW`, `COUNTER/SW`, `COUNTER/SE`) hold the action feedback. They are policy states, not four extra visible bodies. Juri's body/soul and quantum-jump words are retained as model metaphors. The original field impulse remains a separate one-source control with unchanged transfer weights; the bodies do not create four new impulse sources.

At each slider step the bodies may stay, move like a chess king, or, once their own path and free-space state allow, make one bounded child. The selected action scores unvisited positions, free neighbours, separation, inward opportunity, prior feedback, and action exploration. **Field intensity has zero weight in that score.** A separate field suggestion is logged for comparison; when every candidate has zero field signal the suggestion is `NO_SIGNAL`. A child has an episode-qualified identity, a derived counter-role ID, and half of its parent's tracked model charge. The four founding pairs remain identifiable, and the total tracked charge remains four. These quantities are software bookkeeping, not physical matter.

Dragging the slider backward begins a new episode. Previous snapshots and choices remain in the tab, and the next episode keeps learned counter-role feedback. The blue field for a given step is recomputed from the same unchanged kernel. Thus a route can change while that field does not. Reloading the page starts a fresh local session; there is no cross-device memory.

The blue four-neighbour support is a diamond with axial tips at `(0,±step)` and `(±step,0)`. At step 8 those tips reach the outermost cell row of the fixed 17×17 viewport. `experiments/zuse-camera.js` then rotates **only the display projection** toward 45°. This fits the diamond temporarily within the same square. After the rotation limit, the camera scale decreases to show a larger logical region through step 16. The comparison field has an unrotated, separately scaled camera. The readout exposes the rotation and scale; all distribution values and metrics remain in the original lattice coordinates. The fixed dark view guides show four quadrants and subregions; the rotated inner lattice follows the field and bodies. These guides are a bounded recursive layout cue, not a recursive spatial data structure or a claim about physical spacetime.

The old `(step,step)` measurement remains in the metric readout and is shown as a dashed white outline on the comparison canvas. That cell is not a body. The two field rules retain their original mass and variance tests. The added tests cover symmetric starts, paired counter-role IDs, replay and feedback, non-forced choices against field suggestions, bounded cloning, field invariance, camera geometry and Pages packaging. No test establishes consciousness, quantum transport, radioactive colour, a natural learning law or a physical universe model.
